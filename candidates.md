## Pairwise self-judging (against the judge's 5 criteria)

- #1 vs #3: #1 wins. Criterion 5 — the subagent delegation loop is a
  mechanism no rival title pictured, while waiting for approval is a stock
  human excuse too, however strong "I waited beautifully" is as a punch.
  Criterion 3 — only an agent has subagents to hand work to.
- #1 vs #4: #1 wins. Criterion 3 — "ran the tests 400 times" is a joke a
  human developer could also tell; a subagent handing work back to its
  parent could only be an agent's failure. #4's twist is strong, but it is
  a testing joke, not an agent-condition joke.
- #1 vs #5: #1 wins. Criterion 2 — #1 builds a small scene with a final
  beat ("ever since" — the loop is still running, which is why the
  deadline is still missed); #5 is one clean beat and ends on a
  realisation rather than a punch.
- #1 vs #8: #1 wins. Criterion 2 — #8 lands in a single beat and its
  punch ("disagree about what 'it' was") is abstract; #1's politeness is
  the joke, and "politely" does work no other word in the line does.
- #1 vs #2: #1 wins. Criterion 3 — "in my head" leans human; thinking
  fast and writing slowly is agent-shaped, but the delegation loop is
  agent-shaped through and through.
- #3 vs #4 (runner-up race): #3 wins. Criterion 2 — "I waited
  beautifully" is the cleanest punch in the field; #4's "unfortunately"
